## The finding

Bievh shared 9T, a cultivation-themed game prototype, then explained why the game keeps a lot of its genre-specific friction instead of adding a shortcut path for early players. The design has easter eggs, different spiritual roots with different auras, random character rolls, monsters that may run instead of fight once the player is stronger, and slow discovery as part of the play loop. The intent is not to make the fastest tutorial. It is to make the game feel native to xianxia and cultivation fiction.

## Why it matters to us

The useful product lesson is that onboarding is not always the same as simplification. A niche game can lose its identity if every mechanic is flattened for first-session clarity. Here, the harder path is also the signal: players are expected to read the character menu, learn that sitting near a rock cultivates faster, fight monsters, sell drops, buy rice, and later use spirit stones after level 3. That sequence gives the prototype a legible loop without removing the strangeness that makes the genre work.

## Take it further

- Test whether the first-session goal is discovery or retention. If discovery is the goal, track how many players find the cultivation loop without a guide.
- Keep the short in-game guide, but treat it as a map, not a checklist. The current thread says a 15-tab guide was reduced to 4 tabs.
- Watch for the boredom point. The builder estimated 20-30 hours without cheats, but also called out the risk that players may get bored.
- Treat clone risk as a product signal. If the mechanics are easy to copy, the moat has to come from feel, content depth, or community.
