## The finding

The build-club thread moved from "how do we distribute a game in Vietnam" to a small live surface for Little Da Nang. baddeed said they were cooking the landing page again, adding a hidden bar and small games, then shared the Little Da Nang link. The useful part is not the gimmick itself, it is treating the landing page as something people can try, remember, and pass around before store distribution is fully settled.

## Why it matters to us

For small games and experimental products, the first launch surface often matters more than the official channel. A static page can collect clicks, but a playful page can test tone, retention hooks, and whether the concept is legible without asking people to install anything. That fits the team's current question: use the web as the easier distribution path first, then decide what belongs in the store path after the audience signal is less fuzzy.

## Take it further

- Track the landing page as a product surface, not just a marketing asset: what can a visitor do in the first 30 seconds?
- Define the smallest signal worth measuring before store submission, for example repeat visits, shares, or completion of a tiny interaction.
- Keep the hidden-bar and mini-game pieces cheap to change, so the page can teach the team instead of becoming another product to maintain.
- Write down what would make web distribution insufficient, such as payments, retention, platform features, or compliance requirements.
