## The finding

A #build-club thread around a playable browser game prototype quickly split into two kinds of feedback: what blocks a first play session, and what might keep players coming back. baddeed pointed at immediate affordances like lobby state, countdown timing, and the wind angle reference. vincent.console pushed the longer loop: background music, campaign bulletins, referral rewards, rank progression, and an extra resource type for items or weapon gems. The same thread also caught naming risk, visual identity, and distribution questions before the prototype had to pretend it was finished.

## Why it matters to us

Early prototypes are useful when they make feedback cheap enough to sort by layer. The first layer is game feel: does the player understand when the round starts, what the angle means, and what changed after an action. The second layer is retention: campaigns, redeemable cosmetics, progression, and referral loops. Mixing the two too early can hide the real blocker. A player will not care about a wizard hat reward if the first minute still feels unclear, and a builder can waste a sprint polishing growth mechanics before the core loop reads cleanly.

## Take it further

- Run one pass where feedback is tagged as first-session clarity, core-loop feel, retention, or distribution, then only fix the first two before adding more systems.
- Test whether the wind angle needs a visual reference line, a label, or a short pre-shot animation instead of more explanation text.
- Keep the naming question separate from mechanics feedback: catalog approval and searchability are launch constraints, not gameplay constraints.
- For the 2,000-user question, compare community seeding channels against actual retention, not just group size.
