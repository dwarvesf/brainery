These are reading notes on Amy Webb's _The Signals Are Talking_. They are adapted from David Drysdale's Moby Diction notes. Two notes carry the substance. The rest of the original outline survives only as a list of idea titles, which this page keeps at the end.

## The thesis

A strategic inflection point changes the basic assumptions a business runs on, so organizations struggle to picture what it makes possible. The first signs appear on the fringes of a market. Only the people in direct contact with them can see them. The futurist's job is to find which of these patterns will become real trends and which will fade.

## Key ideas

### Inflection points reveal themselves first at the edges

- Organizations need a standing way to find these signs early. Leaders and companies easily become prisoners of their own frame of reference, so they need diverse points of view and ways of thinking.
- Speak with the future. Find the places and people that stand for the strategic shift and learn from their view. Direct exposure to real customers is the best way to see early signs.
- Information must flow from the periphery back to the center.
- To forecast well, remember that the future is woven from threads that may already exist in the present. Seeing them lets us identify and influence possible and probable futures.
- This is uncomfortable. Business incentives make people reluctant to share bad news, which creates blind spots and lets problems fester. Organizations should protect the people who bring bad news.
- Once identified, warning signs should become a standing topic at management review meetings.
- Careful observation and testing of these patterns gives a first-mover advantage to those who act before the fringe becomes the mainstream.

### Our vision of the future is constrained by our view of the past

When we try to predict, the past clouds our vision. We lack the vocabulary and concepts to talk about what is coming. We even struggle to say which patterns of the past or present will carry over.

## The rest of the original outline

The source outline grouped its ideas under four headings. Only the titles remain.

**Trendspotting**

- Trend (foresight).
- Future trends emerge when a fundamental human need is met by a new enabling technology.
- Inflection points reveal themselves first at the edges.
- The genie problem.

**Barriers to spotting trends**

- Change is disorienting because it is constant.
- The paradox of the present.
- Market research.
- Protect dissent. To overcome our "belief bias," we must engage in deliberate disagreement.
- We underestimate the importance of things not directly relevant to us.

**Methods and frameworks**

- Amy Webb's method for finding the future.
- The CIPHER method for trendspotting.
- Think about the future across multiple planes and time zones.
- Market research is a poor tool for trendspotting.

**Communicating about the future**

- Narrative helps the human mind make sense of events. See the [Narrative Economics notes](/narrative-economics-reading-notes) for that idea.

## What to take from it

- Set up a path for fringe signals to reach decision makers, and review warning signs on a schedule.
- Seek out customers and edge users directly. Market research alone is a poor trend detector.
- Make dissent safe. Reward people who bring uncomfortable news.
- Read the past as a limit on your own foresight, not as a map of the future.
