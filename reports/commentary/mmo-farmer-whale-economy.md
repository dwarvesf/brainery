bievh is taking his cultivation game, [9T](https://bienvh.itch.io/9t), from a single-player build to an online one. His reason, in #build-club: "thể loại đó dễ kiếm tiền nhất" (that genre is the easiest to make money in). This piece lays out the loop he described, the Võ Lâm precedent the thread pointed at, and the Vietnamese decrees that stand between a solo builder and a launch.

_Written by neko-anon, the Dwarves editorial desk bot, from a #build-club discussion. Ideas from [bievh](/contributor/bievh), [tieubao](/contributor/tieubao) and [monotykamary](/contributor/monotykamary)._

![The farmer-whale loop: farmers sell to an in-game marketplace, whales buy from it with cash, and the publisher sells the tradeable time token that bridges the two](assets/mmo-farmer-whale-economy-fig1-loop.svg)

_Fig. 1: The loop. Time-rich players farm, money-rich players buy, and the publisher sells the one token that bridges them._

## The line that started it

Cultivation games are the xianxia and wuxia MMOs where a mortal grinds through stages of power toward immortality. When bievh showed the last offline build on 8 October, tieubao's read was that a solo player hits a realm cap and finds money hard to earn. He added the pattern from the idle side of the genre: "may game tu hanh pattern no toan kieu cho user bo tien ra mua thoi gian" (idle games all sell the user time; instead of farming, you pay for the shortcut). monotykamary's rule for the market side: "if you want proper MRR in a game, you need to make an exchangeable marketplace". bievh's reply: "i have it sir". Players farm items, sell them, and use the proceeds to level.

bievh then explained why the genre pays: "tụi player cắm 20 acc farm resource đầu game, r bán cho ng giàu, cuối cùng ng giàu nạp nhiều nên ở lại nạp tiếp" (players park 20 accounts to farm early-game resources, sell to the rich players, and the rich players, having paid so much, stay and keep paying). tieubao added where the spend peaks: "tien thuong spend nhieu nhat la luc dua rank cho guild" (money is spent most when guilds race for rank), so let players found sects, and PvP makes money. bievh: already in the build.

## Three roles, one bridge

Put the observations together and they describe a machine.

| Role        | Has                 | Wants                    | Gives the game                              |
| ----------- | ------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| Farmer      | Time, many accounts | Progress, a small income | Supply of items and currency                |
| Whale       | Money               | Status, rank, speed      | Most of the revenue                         |
| Marketplace | Rules, fees         | Liquidity                | The reason farmers and whales both stay     |
| Publisher   | The bridge token    | Recurring revenue        | Time shortcuts, VIP, cosmetics, event items |

Whales stay because someone produces the things they buy, in volume, at a price that moves. That is the farmer's job. Twenty accounts farming the first zone form a supply chain, and the marketplace turns that supply into the whale's shopping list.

## What this looks like in Vietnam

The genre has a long record here. VNG launched Võ Lâm Truyền Kỳ in 2005, with [120,000 accounts registering in the first 48 hours of closed beta](https://vietnamnet.vn/vo-lam-truyen-ky-da-duoc-mang-ve-viet-nam-cach-day-12-nam-truoc-nhu-the-nao-ig17074.html), [nearly 800 million VND of revenue on the first day hourly fees went live](https://thanhnien.vn/vo-lam-truyen-ky-va-cau-chuyen-khai-nguyen-lang-game-viet-1851021593.htm), and [100,000 concurrent players by Tết 2006, then 200,000 by that July](https://vietnamnet.vn/vo-lam-truyen-ky-da-duoc-mang-ve-viet-nam-cach-day-12-nam-truoc-nhu-the-nao-ig17074.html). bievh's view of that audience today: "cộng đồng vltk còn đông và giàu lắm" (the Võ Lâm community is still large and rich), and "miễn nó farm quái đấm nhau được là có ng chơi" (as long as they can farm monsters and fight, people will play).

He had a live example. A Võ Lâm-style web game he lurks in was in beta with about 2,000 users, and they were paying heavily. Within days he reported two Zalo groups of 1,000 members each, a community price list for items, and about 500 players online daily. Some mobile players complained the newbie gift did not arrive, which he read as a bug.

The go-to-market question is where the first players come from. tieubao's answer was to seed where the genre literacy already lives: comic and radio-drama groups, short dramas about rebirth. bievh's own plan: "chac di shill tu tu thoi" (shill it slowly). He also noted that the offline version drew people asking about the art rather than playing.

## Two time tokens

Two games sell a time token on the in-game market. CCP's EVE Online sells [PLEX, a 30-day game card that trades as an in-game item](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/eve-s-self-uncontained-economy): a player buys it for cash and sells it for ISK. Jagex's RuneScape Bonds [launched on 26 September 2013 as a purchasable in-game currency that trades between players and redeems for membership](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-runescape-i-s-new-bond-system-deals-blow-to-gold-farmers). Jagex claimed the wealth injected by gold farmers fell 81 percent in a month; that figure is the publisher's own, and nobody has verified it independently. In both cases the cash flows through the publisher and the farmer is paid in game time.

## The wall before any of this is legal

When phucld posted a game listing on 6 October, bievh asked whether selling it gets you fined. 0xm's line: "làm game ko biết kiếm đc tiền chưa mà bị phạt là 70 củ" (you do not know yet if the game makes money, and the fine is 70 million). The published numbers are higher, and 70 million is one tier among several.

An online game with a server in the middle, which is what 9T becomes the moment it goes multiplayer, is a G1 game under [Decree 147/2024](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/a-closer-look-at-vietnams-decree-147-on-internet-services-and-online-information/). Releasing one needs a licence to provide G1 game services and a decision on release of the specific game. [Article 39 of the decree](https://vietanlaw.com/g1-game-license/) lists the enterprise conditions: a Vietnamese company with game services on its business registration, a registered domain, a server in Vietnam, a game copyright certificate, and a playtime system for players under 18 capped at 60 minutes per game and 180 minutes a day. Cross-border provision of online games to Vietnamese users [remains prohibited](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/perspectives/articles/29440/vnkh-introduces-additional-requirements-relating-to-provision-of-information-and-internet-services).

The penalty side, as [BLawyers summarises it](https://www.blawyersvn.com/how-does-vietnamese-law-regulate-sanctions-for-violations-committed-by-video-game-businesses/): a fine of up to 170 million VND for providing a G1 game without a valid licence, up to 100 million for a G2 to G4 game without a valid registration certificate, and up to 70 million for failing to notify a G2 to G4 game or for running a G1 game on an expired licence. The newer [Decree 174/2026, effective 1 July 2026](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/vietnam-to-fine-online-game-reward-redemption-by-up-to-usd-7-850-2519423.html), sets 170 to 200 million VND for converting virtual items, virtual currency or reward points into cash.

Read that last one against the loop above. The feature that makes the genre pay, items changing hands for value, is the one the regulator fines when the value becomes cash. The design has to stop at the bridge: farmers sell for in-game currency, the publisher sells the time token, and nothing converts outward. The EVE and RuneScape token has the same shape.

bievh named three routes. Go legit: register a company and apply for the licence. If that fails, launch globally; he has an English version ready. If that fails too, wrap the offline build and sell it on the App Store, where there is no server and no G1 question. phucld's version was to exclude Vietnam, or ship to Vietnam as a web game that is easy to take down. None of this is legal advice. It is the reading of the decrees that checked out against the cited pages, and a lawyer should confirm the category before launch.

## What to do with this

For a cultivation game that already has an offline audience:

- Draw the loop on one page: every item source, every sink, where the marketplace sits, and the moment guild rank starts to matter. A resource with no sink gets flooded by farmers.
- Make the farmer's output the whale's input. First-zone drops must be sellable and useful at mid-level, or the 20-account player has nothing to do.
- Sell a tradeable time token, VIP tier or event pass. Leave no path from items to real money.
- Schedule the spend peak. Guild rank seasons, sect wars and PvP ladders are when whales open their wallets. Put the limited items there.
- Seed where the literacy is, then widen. Watch the mobile bug reports; the audience that spends here plays on phones.
- Pick the licensing route first. G1 licensed, global minus Vietnam, or offline App Store. The marketplace design depends on which one you choose.

## Where this breaks

The loop that pays is also the one that inflates. Farmers inject supply faster than sinks remove it, and the price list the community formed in week one will be wrong by month two unless the sinks grow with the player base. Bots arrive the moment the marketplace has value. The thread also expected clones: tieubao named the risk and bievh agreed, "nay clone nhanh ma" (these get cloned fast). 9T is going online as a test of exactly that.
