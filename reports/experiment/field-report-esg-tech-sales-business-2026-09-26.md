## Field Report: ESG-tech is a sales business, not a software business

Date: 2026-09-26
Channel: Dwarves Discord #ai-tech
Status: DRAFT for memo.d.foundation, not yet published

## TL;DR

- A green-finance (ESG reporting) startup, founded in late 2023, reportedly crossed about $10M within a year and about $25M around two years in. The founder's former colleague described it in passing; the figures are unverified claims, not audited numbers.
- The business is not software-led. It sells ESG reports, audit + compliance work, and sensor installation to banks and manufacturers. Of roughly 80 staff, about 5 are technical. The rest is business development and sales.
- Reading: in this segment, the moat is regulatory tailwinds plus sales trust, not code. For engineers, the defensible layer is the reporting and audit tooling, not trying to out-build the incumbent services model.

## What the thread said

In #ai-tech, a member pointed the channel at the "green finance" domain: their former company started in late 2023 and crossed claimed revenue of about $25M, up from about $10M in early 2025 (about $15M of growth in a year and a half). The business model, as described, revolves around doing ESG audits and installing sensors, with ESG reports, audit compliance and sensors as the product lines. Around 5 of 80 staff are technical; most of the team is business development and sales going after deals with banks and with industries such as garments, lubricants and beverages to produce ESG reports. Another member's honest summary: "hear that and you know there's little tech."

The poster judged the market hot in 2025 (a couple of named clients, many deals) and uncertain for 2026, but framed the end goal as net zero 2050.

Source links for each claim are listed under Sources below.

## Why the 5-of-80 ratio is the detail that matters

A company that sells ESG reporting and audit services does not need many engineers because the product is trust:

- Compliance credibility and audit track record are bought, not coded. Banks and listed-supplier manufacturers outsource reporting because a third-party report is defensible in front of a regulator.
- The recurring revenue is services-led: an audit every cycle, a sensor installed per site, evidence collected per reporting period. Software is the cost center that makes the services cheaper to deliver.
- Regulation is the demand engine. ESG disclosure is being made mandatory across major markets (EU CSRD, California SB 253, ISSB adoption in Japan and Brazil) and Vietnam has committed to net zero 2050 with emerging sustainability reporting rules for listed firms. Where reporting is mandatory, the wall of demand shows up in BD pipelines before it shows up in engineering.

None of this is a knock on the model. It is a warning about where an engineer should position: the margin and the defensibility sit in the tooling layer (data collection, audit evidence, assurance, sensor pipelines) that the 5-engineer core has not had headcount to build well. That is the gap a product team can fill.

## What engineers should take from it

- The opportunity in ESG-tech is workflow tooling around a services business, not replacing the services business.
- Expect software to be the enabler of services revenue, and size engineering expectations accordingly.
- A 5-of-80 technical ratio is a signal that the tooling layer is immature and open.
