a. One Token, One Vote (1T1V)

- Mechanics: 1 DFG = 1 vote, regardless of staking status.
    
- Pros: Simple, aligns with token ownership, easy to implement.
    
- Cons: Vulnerable to whale dominance; large holders (e.g., team with 400,000 DFG) could control outcomes.
    
- Suitability: Initial phase (Q3-Q4 2025) while team holds majority.

- Mechanics: Vote weight = √(DFG held), with cost increasing quadratically (e.g., 1 vote = 1 DFG, 4 votes = 10 DFG).
    
- Pros: Reduces whale influence, encourages broader participation.
    
- Cons: Complex to implement, requires DFG spending, may deter small holders.
    
- Suitability: Mid-term (Q1-Q2 2026) as community grows.
    

c. Delegated Voting (DV)

- Mechanics: DFG holders delegate votes to trusted representatives (e.g., research leads), with optional revocation.
    
- Pros: Leverages expertise, increases engagement, supports decentralization.
    
- Cons: Risk of representative bias, requires trust infrastructure.
    
- Suitability: Long-term (post-2026) with established community.

- Mechanics: 1 DFG = 1 vote, staked DFG = 2 votes (current design).
    
- Pros: Rewards long-term commitment, balances ownership and staking.
    
- Cons: Favors stakers, may exclude non-staking holders.
    
- Suitability: Current hybrid approach, adjustable as staking adoption grows.
